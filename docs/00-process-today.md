# Step 0: how a construction-defect claim is investigated today

Status: approved 2026-09-26. Every statement carries a source. "Unsourced" means we looked
and found nothing reliable; we do not claim it. [vendor] marks sources with a commercial interest in
the problem looking large. [weak] marks a wiki-grade source.

The demo case is the NTSB docket HWY06MH024: the 10 July 2006 ceiling collapse in the I-90 connector
tunnel of Boston's Central Artery/Tunnel project ("Big Dig"). One person died. Casefile quotes the
record; it does not assign fault.

## 2. End user (approved 2026-09-26)

The **liability side: the carrier's claims examiner and its appointed defense counsel.** Reasoning,
not a sourced finding: that side holds its insured's project records from day one, and its core
document task is finding other responsible parties from the records rather than from anyone's
account (JSH). That is a reconstruction problem. The property/subrogation side usually lacks the
records until discovery; it is a later market.

On the Big Dig, every party had this problem at once: the designer (Gannett Fleming), the management
consultant (Bechtel/Parsons Brinckerhoff), the tunnel finishes contractor (Modern Continental), the
epoxy supplier (Powers Fasteners), and the project's wrap-up insurance program (OCIP), which paid
$40M of the 2008 settlement ([Insurance Journal](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/east/2008/01/23/86666.htm)).

## 3. Journey today (liability side)

1. Claim or complaint arrives (first notice of loss); parties identified and service checked. (JSH)
2. Map relationships: designers, GC, prime and lower-tier subcontractors. (JSH)
3. Collect records: contracts, plans, specs, change orders, RFIs, submittals, daily reports, photos,
   inspection records, meeting minutes, correspondence
   ([NY construction law](https://www.nyconstructionlaw.com/construction-defect-claims-and-litigation/),
   [GAR guide](https://globalarbitrationreview.com/guide/the-guide-construction-arbitration/sixth-edition/article/documents-and-other-types-of-evidence-in-construction-disputes)).
   They arrive as project-management exports, email, spreadsheets, large PDFs, photos and messaging
   threads; many formats review software cannot open
   ([Nextpoint](https://www.nextpoint.com/ediscovery-blog/5-ediscovery-challenges-construction-litigation-support/) [vendor],
   [DISCO](https://csdisco.com/blog/ediscovery-best-practices-construction-litigation) [vendor]).
4. Find other responsible parties in the records; tender to their carriers. (JSH, CLM)
5. Coverage position and, if needed, reservation of rights. (CLM, FWHT)
6. Retain the forensic expert, who reviews documents, inspects, tests and reports (see section 7).
7. Set the reserve and adjust it as information develops
   ([Milliman](https://www.milliman.com/en/insight/things-fall-apart-the-long-life-of-construction-defect-liability)).

Deadlines the examiner works under (NAIC model regulation; state adoption varies): acknowledge a claim
within 15 days; reply to communications within 15 days; give unrepresented third parties 60 days'
notice before a limitations period expires; document the claim file
([NAIC Model 902](https://content.naic.org/sites/default/files/model-law-902.pdf)).

## 4. Time and cost

- Lifecycle: reporting lag up to ~6 years; discovery "can take another two years"; ~3 more to settle;
  "15 years from the time a policy is issued to the time a claim against it is settled"; reserves
  "sometimes vary by 50% or more". (Milliman)
- Expert rates, all specialties (SEAK 2021, ~1,100 experts): average $422/hr file review, $524/hr
  deposition, $550/hr trial; no construction breakout in the free summary
  ([SEAK](https://seak.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/2021-EW-Fee-Survey-Summary-Report.pdf)).
- Document review ≈ 73% of e-discovery production cost (RAND, 57 productions, not construction)
  ([RAND](https://www.rand.org/pubs/monographs/MG1208.html)).
- Volumes in large cases: "multiple terabytes" (DISCO), "nearly seven terabytes" (Nextpoint), "five
  million, ten million, or even more documents"
  ([Everlaw](https://www.everlaw.com/blog/case-studies/a-construction-litigation-leader-tackles-its-most-complex-cases-with-everlaw/)). All [vendor].
- "Most construction defect volume desks can handle only three or four diaries a day"
  ([CLM](https://www.theclm.org/Magazine/articles/construction-defect-insurance-claims/366)).
- **How long document review and chronology building take: unsourced.** We will not say "months".

## 5. What they produce

- A chronology, a cast list (dramatis personae) and a glossary, recommended at the outset by the 2019
  ICC report (GAR guide).
- The expert report (section 7).
- Tender letters, coverage position letter, reserve; on the property side, notice letters to targets.

## 6. Pain points (sourced)

- Records hard to find years later ([NFP](https://www.nfp.com/insights/californias-long-tail-problem/)).
- An expert's opinions excluded because he "was not familiar with the contract documents"
  ([ASCE](https://www.asce.org/publications-and-news/civil-engineering-source/civil-engineering-magazine/issues/magazine-issue/article/2021/11/the-perils-of-calling-an-expert-witness)).
- Keyword search fails: "defect", "crack", "change" "will appear in almost every daily log" (DISCO [vendor]).
- Proprietary formats review platforms can't open (Nextpoint [vendor]).

## 7. ASTM and the forensic process

**What ASTM is.** ASTM International writes voluntary consensus standards in about 150 technical
committees balanced between producers, users and general-interest members; changes pass committee
ballots ([ASTM regulations](https://www.astm.org/membership-participation/technical-committees/key-documents/regulations-governing-astm-technical-committees)).
The standards are not law. They become mandatory only when a statute, code or contract adopts them
([ASTM](https://www.astm.org/news/standards-regulations-and-incorporation-reference-nd12)).

**The forensic standards (Committee E30)** describe how an investigator collects information and
reports opinions:

| Standard | What it covers | Status | Relevance to Casefile |
|---|---|---|---|
| [E1188](https://store.astm.org/e1188-23.html) | Collection and preservation of information and physical items by a technical investigator | Active (E1188-23) | Closest fit: collecting and preserving information |
| [E620](https://store.astm.org/e0620-18.html) | Reporting opinions of scientific or technical experts | Active (E620-18) | What the expert's report must show; our cited chronology and documents-reviewed list feed it |
| [E3176](https://store.astm.org/e3176-20.html) | Forensic engineering expert reports; users include courts, attorneys and insurance adjusters; "informational and not mandatory" | Active (E3176-24) | Same |
| [E678](https://store.astm.org/e0678-07r13.html) | Evaluation of scientific or technical data | **Withdrawn 2022** | Do not cite as current |
| [E1020](https://www.astm.org/Standards/E1020.htm) | Reporting incidents that may involve litigation | **Withdrawn 2022** | Do not cite as current |
| [E860](https://store.astm.org/e0860-22.html) | Examining physical items that may be involved in litigation | Active | Physical evidence, not documents |

The relevance column is our reasoning, not a sourced finding.

**ASTM E2128** (evaluating water leakage of building walls) has seven steps: review project documents,
evaluate the design concept, establish service history, inspect, test, analyse, report
([ASTM](https://store.astm.org/e2128-20.html), [IIBEC](https://iibec.org/publication-post/2010-01-haughton-murphy2/)).
It is the method for a water-intrusion claim. **It does not apply to the Big Dig**, which was an
anchor failure. We cite it only to show that forensic method begins with document review.

**Anchor standards relevant to the Big Dig:** [ASTM E488](https://store.astm.org/e0488_e0488m-22.html)
(strength of anchors in concrete) and [ASTM E1512](https://store.astm.org/e1512-01r15.html) (bond
performance of bonded anchors). The NTSB found the epoxy had failed creep tests under ICC AC58 in
1995–96, named the "lack of standards for the testing of adhesive anchors in sustained tensile-load
applications" as a safety issue, and recommended protocols "building on current test standards from
ASTM" ([NTSB HAR-07/02](https://www.ntsb.gov/investigations/AccidentReports/Reports/HAR0702.pdf), pp. 32–33, 99–100).

## 9. Legal frame

- **Repose.** Massachusetts: M.G.L. c.260 §2B (above). For comparison, California allows 10 years from
  substantial completion ([CCP 337.15](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CCP&sectionNum=337.15));
  Florida cut its period from 10 to 7 years in 2023
  ([Bilzin](https://www.bilzin.com/we-think-big/insights/publications/2023/05/changes-to-the-statute-of-repose)).
- **Liability is a legal finding.** It turns on contracts, waivers, repose and coverage terms, and FRE
  704 guards against expert opinions "which would merely tell the jury what result to reach"
  ([FRE 704](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_704)).
- **The NTSB report is not a liability finding.** NTSB investigations are "not conducted for the
  purpose of determining the rights, liabilities, or blame of any person"
  ([49 CFR 831.4](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/49/831.4)), and "No part of a report of the
  Board ... may be admitted into evidence or used in a civil action for damages"
  ([49 U.S.C. §1154(b)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/49/1154)). Casefile uses HAR-07/02 as
  the answer key for its eval and as context. It never presents it as establishing fault.

## 10. What this means for Casefile

- The user is the liability-side claims examiner and defense counsel. Casefile produces what they
  already produce at the start: **a chronology, a cast list, an exhibit list**. It adds what they
  cannot get today: **a coverage report of what was and was not read**.
- The pain points match the design: formats nobody can open (the parser registry), keyword noise
  (search plus verified quotes), experts caught not knowing the documents (every line cited).
- We cannot claim how long chronology building takes today. The pitch uses sourced figures only, or
  our own measurements: documents and pages per case, time for our run.
